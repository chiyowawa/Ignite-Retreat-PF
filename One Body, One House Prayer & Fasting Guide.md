# One Body, One House

A week in Romans 12

Welcome

## Prayer & Fasting Guide

Every family carries many roles and expectations, and with them come disappointments that can quietly create friction. By God’s grace our house has grown, and as leaders we need to pause and ask honestly: *How has my heart been towards my brothers and sisters?* Is it still marked by gratitude, or has it slowly filled with resentment?

Over this week, we will journey through Romans 12, where Paul calls us to *“be transformed by the renewing of your mind”* (v. 2). The word he uses is *metamorphoō*, meaning a deep change from the inside out. God does not ask for a partial commitment. When we offer our whole selves to Him as a living sacrifice, He forms us into one body with many members, a family that loves sincerely, honours one another and overcomes evil with good.

As we pray and fast together, may we not only learn *about* Romans 12, but let God speak to us through it. And may we stay anchored in this truth: our identity is found not in our roles, but in who we are in Christ and who we are becoming in God’s hands.

## Remember Mercy

Romans 12:1 (NIV)

*Therefore, I urge you, brothers and sisters, in view of God’s mercy, to offer your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God—this is your true and proper worship.*

### Read

In the passage, before Paul asks anything of us, he reminds us what God has already done. Even though all of us have disobeyed, God has chosen to show mercy to us all *(Romans 11:32)*. We do not offer ourselves to God to earn His love; we offer ourselves because we have already received it.

When God showed mercy to us, He also adopted us into His family. None of us earned our place in this family. We are all here only because of God’s grace and mercy, and so is the person beside us — including the brother or sister who frustrates us or disappoints us. The patience and kindness God has shown us become the measure of how we treat one another.

To present ourselves as a *living* sacrifice is to place our whole lives before God, day after day, not just the parts we are comfortable giving. For leaders, this includes what others may not see: the hurts we quietly carry and the disappointments we have allowed to settle in our hearts.

When we lose sight of mercy, service becomes a burden and people begin to feel like problems. Gratitude gives way to resentment, and resentment slowly divides a family. But when mercy stays in view, we see one another differently, as fellow children of the same Father, all still being shaped by His hands.

### Reflect

1. Is there any part of my life I have kept back from God?
2. Has my heart grown cold or resentful towards anyone in the house?

## Renew My Mind

Romans 12:2 (NIV)

Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind. Then you will be able to test and approve what God’s will is—his good, pleasing and perfect will.

### Read

Paul says that once our minds are renewed, then we will be able to discern God’s will. Transformation is not simply about changing our outward behaviour, but about allowing God to reshape the way we think.

The world constantly offers us its own standards of what is right, what success looks like and what is worthwhile. Even in our leadership, it can be easy to make decisions based on what seems most efficient, what others expect of us or what we personally think is best. We may become so accustomed to doing things our way that we forget to pause and seek what God actually wants.

Without realising it, these patterns can also shape how we relate to one another. Unmet expectations can quietly train our minds to notice what others are not doing. We take offence more easily, and we start keeping score.

But God calls us to a different way of thinking. He wants to renew our minds so that we see ourselves and others through His eyes. Over time, we start to tell the difference between what simply works and what actually pleases Him. It shows in small ways: how we handle a disagreement, whether we extend grace when we are hurt, how quickly we choose to honour someone instead of criticising them.

This renewal is an ongoing process, and we cannot renew our own minds by trying harder. Our part is to keep placing ourselves before God and His Word, and let Him do the reshaping. As He does, we will increasingly be able to discern His will and live it out in our leadership and relationships.

### Reflect

1. Where in my leadership have I become comfortable doing things my way, without pausing to seek what God wants?
2. What is one area of my leadership or relationships where I need to invite God to reshape my perspective so that I can better discern and live out His will?

## Honour Every Part

Romans 12:3-5 (NIV)

3For by the grace given me I say to every one of you: Do not think of yourself more highly than you ought, but rather think of yourself with sober judgment, in accordance with the faith God has distributed to each of you. 4For just as each of us has one body with many members, and these members do not all have the same function, 5so in Christ we, though many, form one body, and each member belongs to all the others.

1 Corinthians 12:24-27

24while our presentable parts need no special treatment. But God has put the body together, giving greater honor to the parts that lacked it, 25so that there should be no division in the body, but that its parts should have equal concern for each other. 26If one part suffers, every part suffers with it; if one part is honored, every part rejoices with it. 27Now you are the body of Christ, and each one of you is a part of it.

### Read

In a world where people tend to recognise and honour big and visible works and overlook the small things, it is easy for us as leaders to fall into the same mindset. In fact, we may find ourselves measuring our own worth or the worth of others based on worldly standards, seeing people as "less" in the Kingdom of God based on how much they contribute or how well they perform.

However, we need to ask ourselves an honest question: are we seeing God’s Kingdom and His people through the lens of the world, or through His love and grace? *Romans 12:3* calls us not to think of ourselves more highly than we ought, but to have sober judgment. We have all fallen short of the glory of God, and it is only by His grace that we have the privilege of serving Him at all. Paul continues in verse 4-5 that though we are many, we are one body in Christ, and each member has a different function. No role is without a purpose.

Moreover, *1 Corinthians 12:24–27* reinforces this by reminding us to have equal concern for one another, regardless of the role we hold. We are not called to compete with one another. When one part suffers, we all suffer together, and when one part is honoured, we all rejoice together.

This is how God wants His house to be. We notice when a brother or sister is quietly struggling and help carry their burdens. And when someone is honoured or their ministry flourishes, we celebrate sincerely, because their joy is ours too.

So today, if you find yourself judging others through a worldly lens, ask God to help you see His people as He does. And if you feel small or unseen, or feel you must keep performing to prove yourself, be encouraged in knowing that God has a place for you. What you do is dear to Him, and often the unseen, ordinary things are where His love flows through most.

### Reflect

1. In what areas might I be viewing myself or others through the world’s standards rather than through God’s love and grace?
2. How does knowing that we are all different members of one body change the way we view our own roles and the roles of others?
3. Is there someone you could be more intentional in honouring and encouraging this week? If so spend the time to honour and encourage them.

## Steward Your Gift

Romans 12:6-8 (NIV)

6We have different gifts, according to the grace given to each of us. If your gift is prophesying, then prophesy in accordance with your\[[a](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%2012&version=NIV#fen-NIV-28252a)\] faith; 7if it is serving, then serve; if it is teaching, then teach; 8if it is to encourage, then give encouragement; if it is giving, then give generously; if it is to lead,\[[b](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%2012&version=NIV#fen-NIV-28254b)\] do it diligently; if it is to show mercy, do it cheerfully.

### Read

Identical twins may look alike and grow up in the same home, yet they often turn out surprisingly different. Same parents, same upbringing, and still each one has their own personality and strengths.

In many ways, God’s family is the same. We share the same Father and have received the same grace, yet no two of us are gifted exactly the same way. Our gifts may be different, but every one of them matters. They all come from the same grace of God and serve the same purpose: to build up the body of Christ.

With these gifts comes the responsibility to steward what God has entrusted to us faithfully. In particular, Paul highlights that those who lead should do so diligently. Diligence goes beyond simply fulfilling our responsibilities out of obligation or showing up only when we are needed. It means being intentional and wholehearted with what God has entrusted to us, and continuing to give our best despite when things become challenging.

As leaders, it is easy to get caught up in the busyness of serving. There are always deadlines to meet and people to follow up on. Over time, we may find ourselves going through the motions. We may also become discouraged when our efforts go unnoticed, or when things do not turn out the way we hoped.

Yet our service was never meant to be driven by recognition or approval of others. God call us to be faithful with what He has placed in our hands, whether or not anyone sees it. We keep giving our all because, at the end of the day, it is the Lord that we are serving.

Diligence in leadership also reaches beyond our own responsibilities. It means paying attention to the community God has given us. We learn to recognise and appreciate the different gifts within the house, and to make room for others to contribute and grow in what God has entrusted to them. We are not meant to carry the house alone. We serve alongside one another, each playing our part in strengthening the body of Christ.

So what matters most is not how much we do, or how our gifts compare to someone else’s. What matters is how faithfully we steward what God has entrusted to us. May we be leaders who use our gifts wholeheartedly, recognise the gifts in others and remain diligent in building God’s house together.

### Reflect

1. What gifts has God entrusted to me? Have I embraced them, or have I found myself comparing them with what others have?
2. Do I recognise and appreciate the different gifts in my brothers and sisters? Am I making room for others to grow and contribute, or do I tend to rely on myself?

## Keep the Fire Burning

Romans 12:9-13 (NIV)

9Love must be sincere. Hate what is evil; cling to what is good. 10Be devoted to one another in love. Honor one another above yourselves. 11Never be lacking in zeal, but keep your spiritual fervor, serving the Lord. 12Be joyful in hope, patient in affliction, faithful in prayer. 13Share with the Lord’s people who are in need. Practice hospitality.

### Read

Jesus’ unconditional love teaches us that His love is given to us freely by grace, and as we surrender our lives to Him, we are made new. We love one another because He first loved us.

That is why Paul writes *“love must be sincere.”* A fire is known by its warmth, not just its glow. Sincere love is not only seen; it is felt. In the house of the Lord, we serve alongside people of different backgrounds, personalities and experiences. What holds us together is not that we are all the same, but that we have been loved by the same Saviour. So we are called to be devoted to one another as family, to look beyond our own tasks to the needs of others, to honour our brothers and sisters above ourselves.

Paul then says, *“Never be lacking in zeal, but keep your spiritual fervor, serving the Lord.”*

The Greek word here, *zeō*, literally means ‘to boil’. It describes a heart that stays on fire for God. It is very easy for serving to become a routine as responsibilities pile up, and love slowly turns into duty. This is why sincere love and lasting zeal go hand in hand. A single coal pulled out of the fire soon grows cold, but coals kept together stay burning. God places us in this family so that we keep burning together. When one of us grows weary, the others help fan the flame.

For that to happen, we need to be honest with one another. As leaders entrusted to guide God’s people into His presence, it is easy to hide when our own fire is running low. But sincere love is vulnerable. It shares what God is doing in us, admits when we are weary, and lets others draw close enough to help rekindle the flame.

Truth be told, no family is perfect, but the perfect God is the Father of our house. Family is where we embrace one another’s shortcomings, mistakes and imperfections, and keep loving anyway. And as long as we stay close to Him, our source, and close to one another, the fire will keep burning.

### Reflect

1. When did I last spend unhurried time receiving God’s love, rather than serving out of it?
2. Who can I be honest with about how my heart is really doing? What is hindering me from letting them in?

## Choose to Bless

Romans 12:14-21 (NIV)

14Bless those who persecute you; bless and do not curse. 15Rejoice with those who rejoice; mourn with those who mourn. 16Live in harmony with one another. Do not be proud, but be willing to associate with people of low position.\[[c](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%2012&version=NIV#fen-NIV-28262c)\] Do not be conceited.

17Do not repay anyone evil for evil. Be careful to do what is right in the eyes of everyone. 18If it is possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone. 19Do not take revenge, my dear friends, but leave room for God’s wrath, for it is written: “It is mine to avenge; I will repay,”\[[d](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%2012&version=NIV#fen-NIV-28265d)\] says the Lord. 20On the contrary:

“If your enemy is hungry, feed him;\
if he is thirsty, give him something to drink.\
In doing this, you will heap burning coals on his head.”\[[e](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%2012&version=NIV#fen-NIV-28266e)\]

21Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.

### Read

Have you ever been hurt by someone and found yourself replaying it in your head, or quietly avoiding them at the next meeting? Most of us have. When someone hurts us, our natural response is to pull away, or to vent about them to someone else. But in Romans 12:14, Paul asks us to do the opposite: *“Bless those who persecute you; bless and do not curse.”*

So what does it actually mean to bless someone? The Greek word used here means “to speak well of.” It is not just about feeling okay towards the person. It is choosing to speak good over them, even when they have hurt us.

Honestly, this is not something we can do on our own. But the Holy Spirit in us makes it possible. Jesus Himself showed us how. As He hung on the cross, He did not curse the people who put Him there. Instead, He prayed, *“Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing” (Luke 23:34)*. If Jesus could bless in that moment, then by His Spirit, we can learn to bless too. The same mercy He showed us is the mercy we now get to show others.

Paul takes it even further in verses 17-21, *“Do not repay evil for evil”*. We cannot control how others respond to us, but we can choose how we respond to them. Paul also tells us not to take revenge, but to leave room for God. This does not mean pretending the hurt never happened. It means trusting God to deal with what we cannot fix, so that we are free to keep doing good. Paul even says that if your enemy is hungry, feed him. Blessing is practical. Sometimes blessing looks as simple as sending a kind message or greeting the person we would rather avoid.

And here is the beautiful part: blessing brings healing. When we hold on to bitterness, the wound stays open. But blessing gives God room to heal us.

Paul ends this chapter with a powerful reminder: “Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.” Imagine what our house would look like if every one of us chose to bless instead of curse, to speak well of each other instead of about each other.

### Discuss

1. Why do you think it is easier to complain about someone, or withdraw from them, than to bless them?
2. Share about a time someone blessed you when you didn’t expect it, or when you chose to bless someone who had hurt you. What happened?
3. What would it look like for our house to be known as a house that blesses? What is one thing we can start doing together?
